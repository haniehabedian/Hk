5. Interpretation

  
Setting. Each of the m attackers attacks n systems one after the other, and the score obtained at each system is Xi. The total score Sn of one attacker is the sum of these scores, and the m attackers give m independent copies of Sn. In the Bernoulli model Sn is the number of systems that were penetrated and Sn/n is the success rate. In the Rademacher model +1 and −1 represent gains and losses of the attacker, with no advantage for either side.

  
    
    
  • Law of Large Numbers. The average score Sn/n converges in probability to the mean μ of one step (p for Bernoulli, 0 for Rademacher). In the second chart the paths of the averages are pulled towards μ and the spread shrinks like σ/√n. For a large number of systems the success rate of an attacker is therefore almost deterministic.
    • 
    
  • Variance. Var(Sn) = nσ² grows linearly with n, Var(Sn/n) = σ²/n tends to 0, and Var(Sn/√n) = σ² is constant. Dividing by √n is the scaling at which the fluctuations keep a fixed size.
    • 
    
  • Central Limit Theorem. In the Rademacher case Sn/√n converges in distribution to the standard normal law, and the histogram approaches the bell curve as n grows (for small n it is discrete). In the Bernoulli case the mean of Sn/√n is √n · p, which grows, so the score must be centered first: (Sn − np)/√n is approximately N(0, p(1 − p)).
    • 
    
  • Effect of the number of systems n. A larger n makes the average score stable, while the total score spreads in absolute terms like √n (and grows like n in the Bernoulli case because of the drift np).
    • 
    
  • Effect of the number of attackers m. The distribution of the score of a single attacker does not depend on m. A larger m gives a more accurate picture of that distribution (the error of the histogram is of order 1/√m), and the total score of all attackers, which has mean mnμ, becomes more predictable in relative terms, with relative fluctuations of order 1/√(mn).
